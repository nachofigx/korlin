# 03 — Syntax

*[Español disponible en docs/manual-es.md]*

## 1. Constituent order: SVO

Korlin uses **Subject-Verb-Object** (SVO) order, the most common and easiest
worldwide (Spanish, English, Chinese…).

- `mi go-ve a le ho.` — "I go to the house (I see it)."
  `mi  go-ve  a  le  ho`
  I   go-DIR to ART house

## 2. Modifiers BEFORE the head

Adjectives and determiners come **before** the noun (like English/Chinese).

- `le gu pe` = "the good person"
  `le  gu   pe`
  ART good person
- `me ho` = "big house"
  `me   ho`
  big  house

Order: `[determiner] [adjective] [noun]`.

## 3. Prepositions before the noun

As in Spanish/English: `a` (to), `i` (in), `de` (of/from), `kon` (with), `po` (for/by).

## 4. Negation

Two mechanisms — do not mix them.

- **Sentence negation:** free `na` **immediately before the verb**.
  `mi na sa-ve.` — "I don't know (I see it)."
- **Derivational negation:** prefix `na-` **attached** to a root, creating the
  opposite word: `na-gu` = "bad", `na-toro` = "lie".

## 5. Questions

- **Yes/no**: particle `mo` at the end. The question clause carries **no**
  evidential. `tu go mo?` = "do you go?"
- **Interrogatives** go **first** and are written **with a hyphen** (one
  phonological word): `ki-pe` (who), `ki-lo` (where), `ki-ti` (when), `ki-mo` (how).
  `ki-pe e i le ho?` = "who is in the house?"
  `ki` plus a separate noun is the compositional reading (`ki lo` = "what place",
  two accents). The lexical question word is the hyphenated form (`ki-lo`).

## 6. Subordination

`ki` introduces a declarative complement. **Each finite clause carries its own
evidential** (rule 14; there is no exemption for subordination).

- `mi sa-ve ki li an-go-di.` — "I know that he left (I was told)."
  `mi  sa-ve    ki  li  an-go-di`
  I   know-DIR that he PAST-go-REP

A hyphenated interrogative is a different word and a clause boundary (C-36,
rule 20). The subordinate stays finite and keeps its evidential:

- `mi sa-ve ki-pe go-pen.` — "I know who goes (I infer it)."
- `mi sa-ve ki li go-di.` — "I know that he goes (I was told)."

`ki-pe` (interrogative) ≠ `ki` (complementizer). "Know" here is the speaker's
own state (`-ve`, §4.1), not general knowledge.

## 7. Possession

The normal form is the pronoun **before** the noun: `mi ho` = "my house".
`de` is the analytic or emphatic form: `le ho de mi` = "the house of me" (rule 19).

## 8. Alignment

Nominative-accusative **without cases**: subject and object are distinguished by
SVO order (subject before verb, object after). No case marking.

## 9. Final particle order

`[verb+evidential] … [modality] [attitude] [mo]`

One modality and one attitude. Two attitudes do not chain, and two modalities
do not chain (rule 24). For two nuances, one stays at the end and the other is
expressed in the verb.

Unmarked modality (rule 23) is `to` only with a strong evidential (`-ve`, `-di`,
`-sa`). With `-pen`, unmarked modality is `be`. The explicit particle always wins.

- `li go-di ku bu.` — "He left (I was told, doubtful) 😤." One modality, one attitude.

## Summary of rules
1. SVO order.
2. Modifier before head.
3. Prepositions before the noun.
4. Negation: free `na` before the verb (sentence); prefix `na-` (derivation).
5. `mo` at the end for yes/no (no evidential); interrogatives first, hyphenated
   (`ki-pe`, `ki-lo`, `ki-ti`, `ki-mo`).
6. Subordination with `ki`; evidential in each finite clause. `ki-pe` / `ki-lo` /
   `ki-ti` / `ki-mo` open their own clause (C-36).
7. Possession: pronoun before the noun (`mi ho`); `de` is analytic or emphatic.
8. No cases (SVO order).
9. Final particles, one of each, in fixed order:
   `[verb+evidential] … [modality] [attitude] [mo]`. Unmarked modality follows rule 23.
10. Orders: bare verb root, no evidential, no subject (`Go!` = "Go!").
11. Pro-drop applies also with `na` and `mo` (`na sa-ve.`, `go mo?`).
12. Copula `e` carries the evidential (`le ti e-ve gu.`).
13. Conditional `si … so …`: protasis exempt, apodosis carries the evidential.
14. Every finite clause introduced by `ki` carries its own evidential (no exemption
    for subordination); the protasis of the conditional is the exception (rule 13).
